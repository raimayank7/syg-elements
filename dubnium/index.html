Dubnium (Db)

Element 105 of the periodic table: a transition metal, solid at room temperature.

← 104 RutherfordiumPeriodic table106 Seaborgium →

What is dubnium?

Dubnium is a radioactive element made in particle accelerators, a few atoms at a time.

What is dubnium used for?

Research only.

Where the name comes from

From Dubna, the town in Russia that is home to the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research.

Place in the periodic table

Dubnium is element number 105. It sits in period 7 (row 7) of the periodic table, in group 5 (column 5), and belongs to the d-block. Just before it comes rutherfordium (104), and just after it comes seaborgium (106). In the same column, tantalum is above it. Elements in one column tend to behave alike.

Family: Transition metals.

Physical properties

At room temperature dubnium is a solid.

Inside the atom

An atom of dubnium has 105 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 105 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 7 shells: 2, 8, 18, 32, 32, 11, 2. The outermost shell holds 2 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [Rn]7s2 5f14 6d3.

One mole of dubnium weighs 268.126 grams, so a single gram contains about 2.25 × 1021 atoms.

The ring picture is a simple model that only counts the electrons in each shell. Real atoms do not look like this.
