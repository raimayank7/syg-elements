Erbium (Er)

Element 68 of the periodic table: a lanthanide, solid at room temperature.

← 67 HolmiumPeriodic table69 Thulium →

What is erbium?

Erbium is a soft, silvery metal of the lanthanide row. Its salts are pink.

What is erbium used for?

Amplifiers in fibre-optic cables that carry internet traffic, skin and dental lasers, and pink glass.

Where the name comes from

From Ytterby, the village in Sweden.

Place in the periodic table

Erbium is element number 68. It sits in period 6 (row 6) of the periodic table, in the separate row of lanthanides drawn below the main table, and belongs to the f-block. Just before it comes holmium (67), and just after it comes thulium (69).

Family: Lanthanides.

Physical properties

At room temperature erbium is a solid. It melts at 1,529 °C and boils at 2,868 °C, so it stays liquid over a range of 1,339 degrees. Its density is 9.07 grams per cubic centimetre, so a one-litre block would weigh about 9.07 kilograms. By density it ranks 31st among the 96 elements with a measured value.

Inside the atom

An atom of erbium has 68 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 68 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 6 shells: 2, 8, 18, 30, 8, 2. The outermost shell holds 2 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [Xe]6s2 4f12.

Its electronegativity on the Pauling scale is 1.24. This number shows how strongly an atom pulls shared electrons towards itself: fluorine, the strongest, is 3.98. Removing one electron from an atom takes 6.108 electronvolts (about 589 kilojoules per mole). One mole of erbium weighs 167.26 grams, so a single gram contains about 3.60 × 1021 atoms.
