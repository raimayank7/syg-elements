Thulium (Tm)

Element 69 of the periodic table: a lanthanide, solid at room temperature.

← 68 ErbiumPeriodic table70 Ytterbium →

What is thulium?

Thulium is a soft, silvery metal and one of the least common lanthanides.

What is thulium used for?

Portable X-ray sources and some surgical lasers.

Where the name comes from

From Thule, an ancient name for a far northern land.

Place in the periodic table

Thulium is element number 69. It sits in period 6 (row 6) of the periodic table, in the separate row of lanthanides drawn below the main table, and belongs to the f-block. Just before it comes erbium (68), and just after it comes ytterbium (70).

Family: Lanthanides.

Physical properties

At room temperature thulium is a solid. It melts at 1,545 °C and boils at 1,950 °C, so it stays liquid over a range of 405 degrees. Its density is 9.32 grams per cubic centimetre, so a one-litre block would weigh about 9.32 kilograms. By density it ranks 29th among the 96 elements with a measured value.

Inside the atom

An atom of thulium has 69 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 69 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 6 shells: 2, 8, 18, 31, 8, 2. The outermost shell holds 2 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [Xe]6s2 4f13.

Its electronegativity on the Pauling scale is 1.25. This number shows how strongly an atom pulls shared electrons towards itself: fluorine, the strongest, is 3.98. Removing one electron from an atom takes 6.184 electronvolts (about 597 kilojoules per mole). One mole of thulium weighs 168.93422 grams, so a single gram contains about 3.56 × 1021 atoms.
