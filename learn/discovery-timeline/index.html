When was each element discovered?

People have used some elements, such as gold, copper and iron, for thousands of years. Most of the rest were found in a burst of activity between 1750 and 1900, as chemists learned to separate and test substances. Since 1940, every new element has been made by people, in reactors or particle accelerators.

Known since ancient times (11)

carbon, sulfur, iron, copper, arsenic, silver, tin, antimony, gold, mercury, lead.

Before 1700 (1)

phosphorus 1669.

1700 to 1799 (20)

1800 to 1849 (26)

What drove each wave?

  • 1700s: careful weighing and the study of gases revealed hydrogen, nitrogen and oxygen.
  • Early 1800s: electricity from batteries let Humphry Davy split compounds and isolate sodium, potassium, calcium and others.
  • 1860 onwards: the spectroscope showed each element's own coloured lines, which revealed caesium, rubidium, helium and more.
  • 1890s: the noble gases were separated from air, and radioactivity led to polonium and radium.
  • After 1940: elements beyond uranium were made by firing particles at heavy atoms.

Years are from PubChem, with four corrected by us (see About). Sources sometimes differ by a few years, because finding and confirming an element were often separate events.
