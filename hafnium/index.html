Hafnium (Hf)

Element 72 of the periodic table: a transition metal, solid at room temperature.

← 71 LutetiumPeriodic table73 Tantalum →

What is hafnium?

Hafnium is a shiny, silvery metal that is always found with zirconium and is hard to separate from it.

What is hafnium used for?

Control rods in nuclear reactors, heat-resistant alloys for rocket nozzles, and insulating layers in computer chips.

Where the name comes from

From Hafnia, the Latin name for Copenhagen, where it was discovered.

Place in the periodic table

Hafnium is element number 72. It sits in period 6 (row 6) of the periodic table, in group 4 (column 4), and belongs to the d-block. Just before it comes lutetium (71), and just after it comes tantalum (73). In the same column, zirconium is above it and rutherfordium is below it. Elements in one column tend to behave alike.

Family: Transition metals.

Physical properties

At room temperature hafnium is a solid. It melts at 2,233 °C and boils at 4,603 °C, so it stays liquid over a range of 2,370 degrees. Its density is 13.3 grams per cubic centimetre, so a one-litre block would weigh about 13.3 kilograms. By density it ranks 16th among the 96 elements with a measured value. It has the 10th highest melting point of all elements.

Inside the atom

An atom of hafnium has 72 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 72 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 6 shells: 2, 8, 18, 32, 10, 2. The outermost shell holds 2 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [Xe]6s2 4f14 5d2.

Its electronegativity on the Pauling scale is 1.3. This number shows how strongly an atom pulls shared electrons towards itself: fluorine, the strongest, is 3.98. Removing one electron from an atom takes 6.825 electronvolts (about 659 kilojoules per mole). One mole of hafnium weighs 178.49 grams, so a single gram contains about 3.37 × 1021 atoms.
