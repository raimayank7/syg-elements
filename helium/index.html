Helium (He)

Element 2 of the periodic table: a noble gas, gas at room temperature.

← 1 HydrogenPeriodic table3 Lithium →

What is helium?

Helium is a very light gas that does not burn and does not react with other elements. It is the second most common element in the universe, but rare on Earth, where it is collected from natural gas wells.

What is helium used for?

Cooling the magnets of MRI scanners, filling balloons and airships, breathing mixes for deep-sea divers, and leak testing.

Where the name comes from

From the Greek word helios, the Sun. It was seen in sunlight during an eclipse in 1868, before it was found on Earth.

Place in the periodic table

Helium is element number 2. It sits in period 1 (row 1) of the periodic table, in group 18 (column 18), and belongs to the s-block. Just before it comes hydrogen (1), and just after it comes lithium (3). In the same column, neon is below it. Elements in one column tend to behave alike.

Family: Noble gases.

Physical properties

At room temperature helium is a gas. It boils at -268.9 °C, the lowest boiling point of any element, and it does not freeze at normal pressure however cold it gets. It turns solid only when squeezed. One litre of the gas weighs about 0.178 grams at 0 °C and normal pressure. That is lighter than air, which weighs about 1.29 grams a litre.

Inside the atom

An atom of helium has 2 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 2 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 1 shell: 2. The outermost shell holds 2 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is 1s2.

Removing one electron from an atom takes 24.587 electronvolts (about 2,372 kilojoules per mole). One mole of helium weighs 4.00260 grams, so a single gram contains about 1.50 × 1023 atoms.

The ring picture is a simple model that only counts the electrons in each shell. Real atoms do not look like this.
