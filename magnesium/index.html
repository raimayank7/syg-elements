Magnesium (Mg)

Element 12 of the periodic table: a alkaline earth metal, solid at room temperature.

← 11 SodiumPeriodic table13 Aluminium →

What is magnesium?

Magnesium is a light, silvery metal that burns with a very bright white flame. It is common in rocks and sea water, and the green colour of plants comes from chlorophyll, which contains it.

What is magnesium used for?

Light alloys for cars, aircraft and laptops, fireworks and flares, and antacids and other medicines made from its compounds.

Where the name comes from

From Magnesia, a district in Greece where its minerals were found.

Place in the periodic table

Magnesium is element number 12. It sits in period 3 (row 3) of the periodic table, in group 2 (column 2), and belongs to the s-block. Just before it comes sodium (11), and just after it comes aluminium (13). In the same column, beryllium is above it and calcium is below it. Elements in one column tend to behave alike.

Family: Alkaline earth metals.

Physical properties

At room temperature magnesium is a solid. It melts at 649.9 °C and boils at 1,090 °C, so it stays liquid over a range of 440 degrees. Its density is 1.74 grams per cubic centimetre, so a one-litre block would weigh about 1.74 kilograms. By density it ranks 80th among the 96 elements with a measured value.

Inside the atom

An atom of magnesium has 12 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 12 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 3 shells: 2, 8, 2. The outermost shell holds 2 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [Ne]3s2.

Its electronegativity on the Pauling scale is 1.31. This number shows how strongly an atom pulls shared electrons towards itself: fluorine, the strongest, is 3.98. Removing one electron from an atom takes 7.646 electronvolts (about 738 kilojoules per mole). One mole of magnesium weighs 24.305 grams, so a single gram contains about 2.48 × 1022 atoms.

The ring picture is a simple model that only counts the electrons in each shell. Real atoms do not look like this.
