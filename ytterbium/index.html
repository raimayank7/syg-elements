Ytterbium (Yb)

Element 70 of the periodic table: a lanthanide, solid at room temperature.

← 69 ThuliumPeriodic table71 Lutetium →

What is ytterbium?

Ytterbium is a soft, silvery metal of the lanthanide row.

What is ytterbium used for?

Very accurate atomic clocks, fibre lasers for cutting and marking, and small additions to stainless steel.

Where the name comes from

From Ytterby, the village in Sweden.

Place in the periodic table

Ytterbium is element number 70. It sits in period 6 (row 6) of the periodic table, in the separate row of lanthanides drawn below the main table, and belongs to the f-block. Just before it comes thulium (69), and just after it comes lutetium (71).

Family: Lanthanides.

Physical properties

At room temperature ytterbium is a solid. It melts at 818.9 °C and boils at 1,196 °C, so it stays liquid over a range of 377 degrees. Its density is 6.9 grams per cubic centimetre, so a one-litre block would weigh about 6.9 kilograms. By density it ranks 51st among the 96 elements with a measured value.

Inside the atom

An atom of ytterbium has 70 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 70 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 6 shells: 2, 8, 18, 32, 8, 2. The outermost shell holds 2 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [Xe]6s2 4f14.

Removing one electron from an atom takes 6.254 electronvolts (about 603 kilojoules per mole). One mole of ytterbium weighs 173.05 grams, so a single gram contains about 3.48 × 1021 atoms.
