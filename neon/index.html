Neon (Ne)

Element 10 of the periodic table: a noble gas, gas at room temperature.

← 9 FluorinePeriodic table11 Sodium →

What is neon?

Neon is a gas that does not react with anything. Tiny amounts are present in air, and it is collected when air is made liquid and separated.

What is neon used for?

Bright orange-red advertising signs, high-voltage indicators, some lasers, and very cold refrigeration.

Where the name comes from

From the Greek word neos, meaning new.

Place in the periodic table

Neon is element number 10. It sits in period 2 (row 2) of the periodic table, in group 18 (column 18), and belongs to the p-block. Just before it comes fluorine (9), and just after it comes sodium (11). In the same column, helium is above it and argon is below it. Elements in one column tend to behave alike.

Family: Noble gases.

Physical properties

At room temperature neon is a gas. It melts at -248.6 °C and boils at -246.1 °C, so it stays liquid over a range of 3 degrees. One litre of the gas weighs about 0.9 grams at 0 °C and normal pressure. That is lighter than air, which weighs about 1.29 grams a litre. It has the 3rd lowest melting point of all elements.

Inside the atom

An atom of neon has 10 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 10 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 2 shells: 2, 8. The outermost shell holds 8 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [He]2s2 2p6.

Removing one electron from an atom takes 21.565 electronvolts (about 2,081 kilojoules per mole). One mole of neon weighs 20.180 grams, so a single gram contains about 2.98 × 1022 atoms.
