Palladium (Pd)

Element 46 of the periodic table: a transition metal, solid at room temperature.

← 45 RhodiumPeriodic table47 Silver →

What is palladium?

Palladium is a silvery-white metal of the platinum group. It can soak up a large volume of hydrogen gas.

What is palladium used for?

Catalytic converters for petrol cars, electronics, dental alloys, and jewellery.

Where the name comes from

From the asteroid Pallas, which had been discovered shortly before the element.

Place in the periodic table

Palladium is element number 46. It sits in period 5 (row 5) of the periodic table, in group 10 (column 10), and belongs to the d-block. Just before it comes rhodium (45), and just after it comes silver (47). In the same column, nickel is above it and platinum is below it. Elements in one column tend to behave alike.

Family: Transition metals.

Physical properties

At room temperature palladium is a solid. It melts at 1,555 °C and boils at 2,963 °C, so it stays liquid over a range of 1,408 degrees. Its density is 12 grams per cubic centimetre, so a one-litre block would weigh about 12 kilograms. By density it ranks 19th among the 96 elements with a measured value.

Inside the atom

An atom of palladium has 46 protons in its nucleus and, when neutral, 46 electrons around it. The electrons are arranged in 4 shells: 2, 8, 18, 18. The outermost shell holds 18 electrons. Written in full, the electron configuration is [Kr]4d10.

Its electronegativity on the Pauling scale is 2.2. This number shows how strongly an atom pulls shared electrons towards itself: fluorine, the strongest, is 3.98. Removing one electron from an atom takes 8.337 electronvolts (about 804 kilojoules per mole). One mole of palladium weighs 106.42 grams, so a single gram contains about 5.66 × 1021 atoms.

The ring picture is a simple model that only counts the electrons in each shell. Real atoms do not look like this.
